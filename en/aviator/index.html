1xBet Aviator Strategies

Aviator is chance-based, so no strategy "guarantees" wins. A disciplined approach reduces variance and stretches your bankroll. Here are the five most common strategies:

1. Conservative (1.5× – 2× cash-out)

Set AutoCashout to 1.5× or 2× every round. Hit rate is high (around 60–65% of rounds reach 1.5×), but each win is small. Best for long-term bankroll preservation.

2. Fixed Percentage (1–2% per bet)

Keep each bet at 1–2% of total bankroll. With NPR 5,000 balance, that's NPR 50–100 per bet. This protects you from a single losing streak emptying the account.

3. Dual-Bet (Split)

Two bets in the same round: first cashes out at 1.5× (hedge), the second is left to run for 5× – 10×. The first bet provides steady return; the second occasionally lands a big win. This strategy exploits the native dual-bet UI on 1xBet.

4. Fibonacci Progression

After a loss, increase bet size along the Fibonacci sequence (1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8…). After a win, step back two positions. Less aggressive than Martingale, but a long losing streak can still drain bankroll.

5. High-Risk (3× – 10× target)

Few wins, but big when they land. Suited only for players who prioritise excitement over consistency. Allocate just 1% of bankroll to this strategy, with the rest at 1.5× conservative.

Martingale: Why It's Not Recommended

Martingale (doubling your bet after every loss) is the most dangerous approach. A 6–7 round losing streak (statistically possible) hits your bankroll or the NPR 14,000 max-bet cap — either way, your capital is wiped out. Avoid it.

Provably Fair and Cryptographic RNG

Aviator's most important technical aspect — Provably Fair. Each round's outcome is determined by a cryptographic mix of three components:

  1. Server seed: Spribe publishes it in sealed (hashed) form before the round starts.
  2. Client seed: your browser/app generates it randomly.
  3. Nonce: the round sequence number.

The SHA-256 hash of these three determines the crash multiplier for that round. After the round, Spribe publishes the unsealed server seed, and you can verify the hash yourself. If the result does not match, the game is rigged — but this has never been observed in the wild.

Aviator Predictor: Why It Doesn't Work

Seen YouTube or Telegram ads for an "Aviator predictor app" or "signal channel"? All of them are scams. The reason is simple: the server seed is committed (hashed) before each round begins — no third party can read it. The client seed is generated locally on your device. An external predictor has no seed access.

Apps and bots marketed as "predictors" do one thing: scam your money. Sometimes they ship malware that lifts banking credentials from your phone. Remember — if a predictor genuinely worked, the developer would not be selling an app, they would be playing the game and earning millions.
